The brief

What was asked

A fictional company of about 2,000 employees has an IT team buried in triage: tickets without context, duplicates, wrong priorities, and simple questions waiting in the queue.

The hiring process asked for a chat agent (Nexo Assist in the brief, now Farol Desk) that answers questions from the help center, looks up and opens tickets, and hands the conversation to an analyst when needed. On the other side, a console where the analyst takes over with the agent paused, then hands it back or closes it.

The part the brief itself calls the most important: being able to understand, in every answer, what the agent did and why.

Requirements

  • Strict TypeScript; Mastra as the agent framework; any LLM, with the choice explained.
  • Service-desk rules: priority from impact and urgency, deadlines in business hours, no duplicates, reopening within 7 days.
  • A simulated ticket service, separate from the agent, behaving like a legacy system that sometimes goes down.
  • Conversations, tickets and the state of each conversation surviving a restart.
  • Security and privacy (one person's tickets never shown to another) and the cost per conversation under control.
  • Runs locally with a few commands and follows the screen prototype.

01The product

Both sides of the desk

Screenshots of the app with the new brand and made-up data. The app is in Portuguese.

For the employee

Solved without a queue

  • They write the way they talk. The agent searches the help center and asks “Did this solve it?”; the answer feeds the resolution indicator.
  • It finds a ticket without its number and warns when the deadline is at risk (75% of the business-hours deadline), showing the math.
  • It builds a draft with priority and deadline computed in code. Nothing is created before the person confirms; “Change details” recalculates on the server.
  • When three or more people report the same problem within 4 business hours, it raises the impact to “team” without revealing who reported it.
  • High frustration brings up a “Talk to an analyst” button. An explicit request, a critical case, an overdue ticket or doubt that persists hands the conversation over.

por quê?

Every answer opens its why

The first step of the model is forced to record its understanding, through the recordUnderstanding tool; that is where the panel comes from. Any decision can be audited in the interface itself and in the analyst console.

The “por quê?” (“why?”) panel on every answer shows:

  • intent
  • confidence
  • reason
  • tools, with input and output
  • rules that fired
  • model
  • tokens
  • time

For the analyst

Arriving with context

  • A queue with signals: high frustration, possible mass incident, reason for the handoff.
  • The conversation report: what the person needs, what the agent already did and what is pending. It is built from the history and the traces, without calling the model.
  • Takes over with the agent paused, hands it back or closes it. The database only accepts the change if the state is still the expected one, so analyst and agent never step on each other.
  • “Suggest a reply”: the model writes, the analyst reviews before sending.
  • Everything in real time over the SSE stream, with polling as the fallback. When an analyst takes over a conversation the agent was handling, the person's chat changes at once.

Indicators

Honest indicators

Confirmed resolution kept apart from presumed, not counting open tickets or people who came back with the same problem, and help-center gaps with personal data masked.

A photo of the problem

From photo to analyst

The person sends a photo or screenshot of the problem. A low-cost vision model describes the image, and the agent only gets the description, treated as data and never as an instruction. Only the person and the analysts can see the image.

3 Nov test. In the local demo, Ana attached a photo of an open printer. The agent identified crumpled paper, asked about a message on the control panel and suggested checking the power. The power check was generic for the visible problem. Ana chose to leave the open machine alone and asked for help. The conversation moved to the analyst queue. The test ended there, with the repair still open.

02Inside

The system plan

The same structure as the repository's src/.

  1. 01

    Layers

    Lean DDD. Dependencies only point inwards: app/, agent/ and infrastructure/ depend on application/, which depends on domain/. The domain knows nothing about Next, Mastra or the database, so priority, deadlines and reopening are tested without mocks. Infrastructure implements the application's ports, and a single file wires the pieces together (container.ts).

  2. 02

    Agent and model

    A Mastra agent with 11 tools. The first step is forced to record its understanding (intent, confidence and reason), and code decides which tools exist at each step. The model is DeepSeek-V4-Flash, through DeepInfra, with a daily and a total spend cap: when the cap runs out, the chat says so instead of spending. The trace and the telemetry in the analyst console say which model answered.

    • recordUnderstanding
    • searchHelpCenter
    • askDiagnosticCheck
    • listMyTickets
    • getTicket
    • draftNewTicket
    • draftTicketUpdate
    • confirmDraft
    • cancelDraft
    • offerQuickReplies
    • transferToAnalyst
  3. 03

    Data

    Drizzle on libSQL: SQLite in development, Turso when deployed. Conversations, messages with their trace, drafts and tickets survive a restart. The legacy ticket system is simulated by a decorator that fails 15% of calls; the service retries without creating duplicates. The search embeddings are cached in the database.

  4. 04

    Real time

    The chat receives each turn as NDJSON: steps, cards and text. The analyst console, and the chat while an analyst is attending, open an SSE stream. The server checks a cheap database signature every second and says when something changed. If the stream drops, the screen falls back to polling on its own.

  5. 05

    Optional

    OpenTelemetry tracing with the GenAI conventions, ready for Langfuse, and OpenID Connect SSO (PKCE, state, nonce, JWKS). Both ship turned off and were tested against local servers that follow the protocols, not against real Langfuse, Entra ID or Okta.

From commit to live

Vercel, in production

The app is live on Vercel, with the database on Turso, as a public demo since 29 Oct 2026. Cost is capped in code: up to 100 messages a day across all visitors, per-person limits and a total ceiling, all reserved in the database before the model is called. Each release comes from a git archive of the latest commit, so nothing outside git is uploaded.

git · main→deploy-prod.ps1→Vercel + Tursolive

Docker, CircleCI and Komodo

The Docker image (Node 24, non-root, database on a volume) is 486 MB and passed all 25 smoke-test checks: it migrates, seeds the sample data, logs in for real, checks permissions and restarts without losing data.

CircleCI runs types, lint, tests, E2E and the same smoke test, then publishes the image to Docker Hub. Komodo checks the digest of latest every 10 minutes and redeploys. That path has run since 29 Oct 2026: on every push to main with green CI, Komodo pulls the new image within 10 to 20 minutes. CI never publishes to Vercel.

git · main→CircleCI→Docker Hub→Komodorunning

On its first run, on 28 Oct 2026, CircleCI caught an error my machine was hiding: the LayoutProps type that Next 16 generates in .next/ does not exist in a clean clone. The fix was to run next typegen before tsc. The image is on Docker Hub, and Komodo runs it only on my machine, at 127.0.0.1:3300.

03Decisions

What I chose, and why

As in the product, every decision has a “why?” (“por quê?” in the app): it opens what I understood, what I did and which rules stayed.

D-01model

From Gemini to GPT-6 Luna and DeepSeek, decided by measurement

The production model has been DeepSeek-V4-Flash, through DeepInfra, since 2 Nov. Each switch only happened after measuring the candidate in the evals.

7 models · 24 conversations per model · Luna 26× cheaper, same quality · since 2 Nov, DeepSeek-V4-Flash: 36/36 for US$ 0.07

why?
Understood

I picked Gemini for reliable tool calling (including the tool forced on the first step, which the “why?” panel depends on), low latency with reasoning set to minimal, and a cost of about one US cent per answer. It was a qualitative choice.

Did

I replaced the qualitative choice with data: I ran the production agent with 7 models, measuring effectiveness, quality (G-Eval with 2 judges), latency and real cost, under a US$ 1.80 cap. GPT-6 Luna tied with Gemini within the confidence intervals and cost 26 times less.

I kept Gemini while it had credit. When the Google credits ran out, on 29 Oct, I switched to Luna with one environment variable, after confirming it in the evals: 36/36.

On 2 Nov the OpenRouter credit ran out and the public demo was left without a model. I switched to DeepSeek-V4-Flash, through DeepInfra, again after measuring: 36/36 in the evals for US$ 0.07 for the whole run.

Rules that stayed

With it came a spend ledger, with a daily and a total cap, so US$ 3 lasts at least three weeks. When the cap runs out, the chat says so instead of spending.

D-02search

Search that knows when an article does not apply

The help center is searched by meaning (embeddings) and by keywords, and a cheap model says whether the article it found applies. With no article that applies, asking is better than a wrong answer.

81 questions: 56 → 78 right · wrong article: 22 → 0 · frozen set: 9/18 → 18/18 · US$ 0.00025 per search · without the judge, falls back to the margin

why?
Understood

A look-alike article leads the agent to a wrong answer, and asking is better.

The diagnostic articles exposed the limit of embeddings: they find the topic, not whether the article applies. “I'd like a wireless keyboard” landed on the repair article, and “the fan is making noise” got “Computer won't turn on”.

Did

I measured it on 50 questions written the way an employee would, including questions with no right article. A set of 17 was written only after the choice, to check it was not overfitted. On that set the overall score tied, and the report says so.

Search became two steps, as in Corrective RAG: hybrid search brings 5 candidates and a cheap model says which one applies, which one maybe does, or none. Before fixing it, I wrote 18 request × fault × not-covered questions and froze them in a commit. I compared rerankers, thresholds and a judge over the whole catalog.

Rules that stayed

A threshold only worked at one exact value, so the categorical decision stayed: applies, maybe or none. Without the judge, search falls back to the margin: when the top result does not stand out from the second, it returns nothing.

D-03guarantees

Rules in code, not in the prompt

The model decides what to do. Whatever must be guaranteed lives in tested code, and the prompt only handles how to talk.

step-policy.ts · domain/priority.ts · domain/business-time.ts · draft-service.ts

why?
Understood

A malicious text can change what the model wants to do, and a misread prompt must not be able to break a service-desk rule.

Did

Which tools exist at each step is decided in code, following the action-selector pattern: the model only picks among them. The malicious text changes what it wants to do, but not what it is able to do.

Rules that stayed

Priority from the impact × urgency matrix, deadlines in business hours, mandatory and atomic confirmation, reopening only within 7 days, and identity taken from the signed cookie, never from a model argument.

D-04measurement

Honest evals

The evals check the database state, not only the text of the answer, and the result is published as it came out, even when it is low.

database checked · attacks × 3 · provider failures kept apart · pass^1 0.72 → 0.93 and pass^3 0.50 → 0.80 after the fixes

why?
Understood

When Gemini returned a 503 inside the stream, an attack could “pass” without the model having answered. And there were failures the fixed-case evals did not catch.

Did

I built the simulated employee (pass^k, as in τ-bench): a model plays the employee. In the first round it found four real bugs, such as opening a ticket from a quick reply that was not a “yes”. Each one became a rule in code with a test, not a prompt tweak, and the next round was measured the same way.

Rules that stayed

Each attack runs 3 times, and passing 1 out of 3 counts as a failure. Provider failures are counted separately. The simulated employee is published with the result it gave, even when it is low.

Three defects, found and fixed

  1. E2E would not start on this machine.The test OIDC provider used port 3200, which is Grafana Tempo's default port; with the observability stack running in WSL, listen failed with EADDRINUSE while nothing showed up in Windows netstat. The E2E ports are now configurable (E2E_PORT, E2E_IDP_PORT) and the provider's default moved to 3210.
  2. The analyst's suggested reply bypassed the spend cap.It called the model without going through the dollar cap and without recording the cost. It now checks the cap first and records the cost from the tokens (two new tests).
  3. An ambiguous button in the diagnostics.“Já estava assim” (“It was already like that”), in reply to a yes-or-no question, did not say whether the check was right or wrong. It became “Já estava certo” (“It was already right”); the old label is still accepted in recorded conversations.

Security review of the image attachment

Done by a separate reviewer, read-only, over the image-attachment code, which had been in production since 3 Nov. Nothing critical, but the high finding applied to the public demo until this fix.

What was correct: access control on the image (the conversation's owner and an analyst in the same space; anyone else gets the same 404), type checked from the first bytes, the image description enters the history marked as untrusted data and cannot confirm a ticket, no secret in code or in an error response, migration 0009 is additive.

Findings: 1 high, 2 medium and 4 low
SeverityFindingFix
HighThe image limit was per conversation only. In the public demo, opening new conversations reset the count, and the database could be filled (up to 1 MB per message).Two atomic counters checked before writing: 10 images per person per day and DAILY_SCREENSHOT_LIMIT for the whole day (default 100). Three new tests.

07Still open

What is left

  • What the recording revealed

    The time-clock ticket took six rounds of questions, and the handoff summary included a detail Carla had not reported. The third point, the “Já estava assim” button, was fixed on 11 Nov.

  • What the simulated employee caught

    Every bug from the three rounds became a rule in code, with tests: confirmation must be explicit (and a “Sim, é esse mesmo”, “yes, that’s the one”, that only identifies the ticket reopens nothing), a different problem gets a new ticket, Outlook is filed under E-mail, an analyst is offered only with a reason (a deadline that misses the need, an overdue ticket or frustration) and the workaround question comes before the priority. Re-run after the 29 Oct fixes, senha-sap and vpn-reuniao passed 3 out of 3, and so did the reopen eval. What remains is the model’s own variance: in the full suite on 29 Oct, one mass-incident scenario failed once and passed 3 out of 3 when repeated.

  • Limits of the 11 Nov review

    Image reading was not exercised with the real model in that round, only through tests with a test double and through E2E, which does not call the model. The platform's body limit (Vercel) was not measured; the app's was unit-tested.

  • Image delivery is local only

    The CircleCI → Docker Hub → Komodo path runs, but Komodo lives on my machine, on loopback only: it proves image delivery, it is not a second public environment. Production stays on Vercel, published by hand.

  • Small sample

    10 personas × 3 runs is too few to pin down a rate. The numbers show the direction, not a guarantee.

  • Spend cap

    The public demo has US$ 0.07 a day for the model. When it runs out, the chat says so and comes back the next day, with no fallback model. A conversation solved without an analyst costs about US$ 0.005 in model calls. Since 11 Nov the reply suggestion goes through the cap and records its cost; the embeddings and the relevance judge are still outside the spend ledger, at less than US$ 0.001 per conversation (cost report, in Portuguese ↗, scaling plan ↗).

  • Repeated calls

    Tracing showed the model calling the understanding tool several times in parallel on the first step. Only the first one counts, but the others spend tokens.

  • Evals outside CI

    They run by hand because they call the real model. The next step is running them in CI with a minimum threshold.

  • Real services

    SSO was only tested against a fake provider, and tracing against a local collector. Logging out does not revoke the JWT on the server, and the console needs a wide screen.

  • Material with the old brand

    The demo video, the image-reading animation and the screenshot from the printer test predate 11 Nov and still show the Nexo Assist name.
